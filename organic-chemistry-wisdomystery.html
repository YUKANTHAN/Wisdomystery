About this Book
A compact companion for competitive-exam chemistry.

This volume gathers the core of organic chemistry into a single, turnable reference. Each chapter builds on the one before it — from the language of nomenclature, to the geometry of isomerism, to the invisible intermediates and electron movements that govern every reaction you will meet in the examination hall.

What you will find inside

  • Systematic IUPAC naming rules with worked examples and an interactive checker.
  • Every major type of isomerism, with the classic examples that JEE and NEET repeat.
  • Carbocations, carbanions, radicals and carbenes — and why 3° beats 1°.
  • Inductive, resonance, hyperconjugative and electromeric effects, side by side.
  • SN1/SN2, E1/E2, addition, rearrangement and redox, with the conditions that decide them.
  • The named reactions most often tested, each on its own page.
  • A quick quiz to check yourself, and a final revision summary.

Systematic naming rules for organic compounds — the language of chemistry.
Golden Rule IUPAC name = Substituents (prefix) + Longest chain (root) + Degree of unsaturation + Principal functional group (suffix)

Alphabetical listing rule

Substituents are written in alphabetical order, ignoring multiplying prefixes (di-, tri-, tetra-). Identical groups use di-/tri-/tetra-; complex substituents are placed in parentheses and numbered from the point of attachment.

Example: 3-ethyl-2,4-dimethylpentane — ethyl (e) precedes methyl (m), even though “di-” appears in the name.
Numbering tie-breaker Number from the end giving the lowest locant to the principal group first. On a tie, prefer the substituent that comes first alphabetically.

Step-by-Step Naming Guide
Five decisions, in order, produce every correct IUPAC name.
1
Identify the principal functional group

Use the seniority order. This group determines the suffix of the name.

2
Select the longest carbon chain

Choose the chain that contains the principal group and has the most carbons. This is the parent chain (root word).

3
Number the chain

Start from the end that gives the lowest locant to the principal group. On a tie, give the lowest locant to the alphabetically first substituent.

4
Name the substituents

List them in alphabetical order with locants; use di-, tri-, tetra- for identical groups.

5
Construct the full name

Locant-substituent(s)-root-locant-suffix e.g. 3-methylpentan-2-ol

Worked example CH₃CH₂CH(Cl)CH₃ → chain of four → butane → Cl at C2 → 2-chlorobutane. The C2 carbon is chiral — optical isomers are possible.
