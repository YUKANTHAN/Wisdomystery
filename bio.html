Biomolecules in Chemistry

Biomolecules are chemical compounds produced by living organisms. They form cellular structures, store and transfer energy, carry hereditary information, and regulate the reactions required for life.

The four major classes are carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. Most are built mainly from carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, nitrogen, phosphorus, and sulfur.

Their properties depend on molecular structure and functional groups. Small repeating units called monomers can join through condensation reactions to produce larger polymers, while hydrolysis can break many of these molecules into smaller units.
